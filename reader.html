Chapter Eighteen
The Life and Lies of Albus Dumbledore

The forest was silent around them as Harry stared at the grave. He had not thought about Dumbledore's grave since he had come to visit it that night when Dumbledore had died, had lain in the white tomb on the grounds of Hogwarts. Now the place felt different somehow.

Hermione stood just behind him, not speaking, but he could feel her presence as he could always feel her presence, like a warm anchor in the darkness of the night. Ron was somewhere to his left, his feet crunching slightly in the frost.

"He trusted us," Harry said at last, his voice barely more than a whisper. "He left us all those clues and trusted us to figure it out."

"He left you those things because he believed in you," Hermione said softly. "He always believed in you, Harry."

The wind picked up and rattled through the bare branches above them, sending a shower of ice crystals drifting down around their huddled figures. In the distance, an owl called once, twice, then fell silent.

"We should keep moving," Ron said. The cold had made his voice rough. "They could still be tracking us."

Harry nodded, but did not move at once. There was something pulling at him, something about this place that made him reluctant to leave. He pressed his hand flat against the cold stone of the grave and felt, very faintly, the magic that still lingered there — residue of a great life, a great man, reduced now to memory and cold marble.

"Right," he said at last, pulling his hand away. "You're right."

They Disapparated with three sharp cracks and left the churchyard empty, the moonlight falling silver and indifferent on Dumbledore's tomb, on the fresh-turned earth of Bathilda Bagshot's grave nearby, on the gravestones of James and Lily Potter, on the small plaque that read simply: The last enemy that shall be destroyed is death.

Three streets away from the churchyard, they reappeared in a narrow alley smelling of refuse and damp stone. Harry pressed his back against the cold brick wall and breathed hard, his heart hammering.

"I'm fine," he said before either of them could ask. "I'm fine. Let's find somewhere to set up camp."

Hermione had already unzipped her beaded bag and was rummaging inside it. Ron stood watch at the entrance to the alley, his wand held loosely at his side.
